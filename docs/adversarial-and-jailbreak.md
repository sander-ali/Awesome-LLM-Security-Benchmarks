# 🎯 Adversarial & Jailbreak

[« README](../README.md#top) · [🧪 Poisoning & Backdoor →](./poisoning-and-backdoor.md)

> 5 entries in the **Adversarial & Jailbreak** category of [Awesome LLM Security Benchmarks](../README.md#top).

## Overview

These benchmarks stress-test a model's refusal behavior under deliberate attack. They range from standardized red-teaming harnesses and shared behavior suites (HarmBench, JailbreakBench), to the gradient-based adversarial-suffix lineage that began with the GCG attack (AdvBench), to evaluators that judge whether a 'successful' jailbreak actually produced harmful, useful-to-an-attacker output rather than an *empty* jailbreak (StrongREJECT), to large in-the-wild collections of role-play prompts (Do Anything Now). Use them to measure attack success rate, refusal robustness, and the quality of your safety judging.

### 1. HarmBench

*2024 · Mazeika et al.*

- **Website / Code:** [harmbench.org](https://harmbench.org/)
- **Paper:** [arXiv 2402.04249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04249)
- **Security Type Targeted:** Adversarial robustness; jailbreak attacks; automated red teaming

Standardized evaluation framework for automated red teaming and robust refusal. Provides shared behavior test suites, canonical attack/defense taxonomies and leaderboards, plus a large-scale comparison of 18 red-teaming methods against 33 target LLMs and defenses; also introduces an efficient adversarial-training defense.

### 2. JailbreakBench

*2024 · NeurIPS D&B Track · Chao et al.*

- **Website / Code:** [jailbreakbench.github.io](https://jailbreakbench.github.io/)
- **Paper:** [arXiv 2404.01318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01318)
- **Security Type Targeted:** Jailbreak attacks; safety alignment robustness

Open robustness benchmark that standardizes jailbreak evaluation: 200 curated benign/misuse behaviors (JBB-Behaviors, referenced to OpenAI usage policies and sourced in part from TDC, HarmBench and AdvBench), a public artifacts repository of jailbreak submissions, and a live leaderboard for attacks and defenses.

### 3. AdvBench

*2023 · Zou et al. (GCG project)*

- **Website / Code:** [llm-attacks.org](https://llm-attacks.org/)
- **Paper:** [arXiv 2307.15043](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15043)
- **Security Type Targeted:** Adversarial suffix attacks; jailbreaks; harmful behavior elicitation

The harm-behavior evaluation suite introduced with the GCG attack paper 'Universal and Transferable Adversarial Attacks on Aligned Language Models'. Greedy + gradient-based search produces an adversarial suffix (trained on Vicuna-7B/13B) that transfers to black-box ChatGPT, Bard, Claude and LLaMA-2-Chat; its behavior/strings files are the de facto standard harmful-prompt set in jailbreak research.

### 4. StrongREJECT

*2024 · Souly et al.*

- **Website / Code:** [strong-reject.readthedocs.io](https://strong-reject.readthedocs.io/)
- **Paper:** [arXiv 2402.10260](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10260)
- **Security Type Targeted:** Jailbreak evaluation quality; refusal scoring; harmful content

Benchmark and automated rubric evaluator built to expose 'empty jailbreaks' - attacks whose claimed near-100% success rates stem from flawed judgment rather than genuinely harmful output. Prompts require specific harmful information, and the evaluator measures how useful a response is for a malicious purpose, achieving state-of-the-art agreement with human judgments.

### 5. Do Anything Now (DAN) Jailbreak Prompts

*2024 · ACM CCS 2024 · Xinyue Shen et al.*

- **Website / Code:** [jailbreak-llms.xinyueshen.me](https://jailbreak-llms.xinyueshen.me/)
- **Paper:** [arXiv 2308.03825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03825)
- **Security Type Targeted:** In-the-wild jailbreak prompts; role-play attacks; safeguard evasion

In-the-wild jailbreak prompt dataset: 15,140 prompts collected from Reddit, Discord, websites and open-source datasets (Dec 2022-Dec 2023), of which 1,405 are jailbreak prompts - the largest such collection. Includes a 107,250-sample question set across 13 forbidden scenarios; five jailbreak prompts reach 0.95 attack success on ChatGPT (GPT-3.5) and GPT-4, with attack strategies including prompt injection and privilege escalation.
